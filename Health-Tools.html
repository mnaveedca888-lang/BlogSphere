How SmartFit works

SmartFit uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation, the formula most often used to estimate resting energy use in adults. It takes your weight, height, age and sex and estimates your basal metabolic rate (BMR), the calories your body uses at rest. It then multiplies that by an activity factor, from 1.2 for a mostly sedentary day up to 1.9 for very intense training or a physical job, to give your total daily energy expenditure (TDEE), the calories you use in a typical day.

Your goal then adjusts the target. Maintaining weight keeps the TDEE figure. Gradual weight loss subtracts 300 calories a day, and gradual weight gain adds 250. The macro split shown with your results is 25 percent protein, 45 percent carbohydrate and 30 percent fat. The hydration range is a starting estimate of 30 to 35 millilitres of water per kilogram of body weight per day.

What your results mean

BMI groups adults into four broad ranges. They are screening categories, not a diagnosis, and they do not account for muscle mass, age, sex or where fat is carried on the body.

BMIAdult category
Below 18.5Below the healthy range
18.5 – 24.9Healthy weight range
25.0 – 29.9Above the healthy range
30.0 and aboveHigh BMI range

A calorie target is also a starting point, not a prescription. Two people with the same numbers can have quite different needs, because muscle mass and daily movement vary so much. If the number surprises you, or if you plan a big change to how you eat or train, it is worth checking in with a qualified professional first.

Frequently asked questions

What is BMI and how is it calculated?

BMI, or body mass index, is your weight in kilograms divided by your height in metres squared. For adults, the World Health Organization classes a BMI of 18.5 to 24.9 as the healthy range. BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis, because it does not measure body composition.

How accurate is the SmartFit calorie estimate?

SmartFit estimates your resting energy use with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation and multiplies it by an activity factor to give your total daily energy expenditure. It is a useful starting estimate, but real needs vary with muscle mass, body composition and day-to-day activity, so treat the number as a guide, not an exact target.

Do I need to sign up or share my data to use the calculator?

No. The calculator runs entirely in your browser. Nothing you enter is sent to a server or stored, and there is no account or sign-up.

What is a healthy weight for my height?

A healthy weight for an adult is the weight that puts their BMI between 18.5 and 24.9. Use the Ideal Weight Range Calculator further up this page: enter your height and it shows the matching weight range in kilograms.

Should I follow the calorie target exactly?

No. The target is an illustrative starting point for a typical adult. If you have a medical condition, are pregnant or breastfeeding, are under 18, or take medication that affects weight, speak to a qualified healthcare professional or registered dietitian before changing how you eat.
